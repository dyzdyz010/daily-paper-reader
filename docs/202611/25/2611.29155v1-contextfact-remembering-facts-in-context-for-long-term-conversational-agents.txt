## ABSTRACT 

Agents built on large language models must maintain continuity across repeated interactions with users. However, long-term conversations scatter facts across turns and sessions, making it difficult to retrieve the right evidence with enough context. Memory for such conversations must therefore preserve facts with their context while keeping evidence selectively accessible. We first study how mem-ory construction and retrieval-unit boundaries affect answerability and evidence access. Reading the entire memory bank reveals gaps that cannot be resolved by retrieval selection alone, showing that some context must be preserved dur-ing memory construction. Adjacent-turn grouping improves evidence access at matched unique-turn budgets, while wider windows do not consistently help. This motivates keeping fact-specific context local while organizing cross-session evi-dence separately. We propose ContextFact, which links context-preserving fact memories with source-grounded entity representations. Within sessions, it gathers the context needed to interpret each fact. Across sessions, it aligns recurring refer-ents and revisits the source dialogue to reconstruct coherent entity representations linked to the supporting facts. At query time, ContextFact retrieves fact and entity views, reranks them independently, and uses entity-linked expansion to surface re-lated facts; complete entity representations then provide organized cross-session evidence. With Qwen3.5-27B as the backbone, ContextFact achieves state-of-the-art performance while using only 21% of the evidence tokens consumed by the strongest evaluated baseline, obtaining 86 .49% overall judge accuracy on Lo-CoMo and 91 .00% on LongMemEval-S. Controlled ablations support context-preserving fact boundaries and source-grounded entity reconstruction, while entity readout improves multi-hop accuracy beyond adding more facts. Code is available at https://github.com/JiayuHuang01/ContextFact .

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language models are increasingly deployed as personal agents that interact with the same users across many conversations. Such agents must preserve user context over time without requiring users to repeatedly restate their preferences, experiences, decisions, and plans (Maharana et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025; Zhong et al., 2024; Packer et al., 2023). The evidence needed for a response often spans multiple turns and sessions: a plan may be introduced in one conversation, qualified or revised in another, and referred to again later. Simply retaining the entire history is not sufficient, because models can struggle to locate and use relevant information in long contexts (Liu et al., 2024). Long-term memory must therefore preserve the meaning of past interactions while exposing a compact, relevant set of evidence when needed. Existing systems construct memories through fact extraction (Chhikara et al., 2025), topical segmen-tation (Pan et al., 2025), or contextualized events (Zhou & Han, 2025), and organize them through linked representations (Xia et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2026). Yet a central question remains: what information should stay together in a memory, and how should related evidence remain selectively accessible? We investigate this question on LoCoMo (Maharana et al., 2024) with two controlled di-agnostics. First, even when the model reads each system’s complete memory bank without retrieval,

Yet reading the entire dialogue is not ideal either: an oracle that reads only the sessions containing annotated evidence improves multi-hop and temporal accuracy. Second, under a fixed turn budget, grouping adjacent turns improves single-hop evidence access, but wider windows bring no consistent further gain. Together, these findings motivate a design principle: memory units should preserve the con-text needed to interpret each fact, while keeping distinct facts independently retrievable and evidence across sessions selectively accessible. Guided by this principle, we propose ContextFact , which preserves the context of each fact and connects related facts across sessions through the entities they describe. Within each session, Con-textFact builds one memory around each fact, gathering the context needed to interpret it, such as its reasons or outcome, even when that context appears in nonadjacent turns. Further, to bring together information about the same entity (e.g., a person, project, or object) scattered across sessions, Con-textFact constructs an entity view . During fact extraction, it also creates session-level entity entries, each linked to the fact memories that describe that entity. It aligns these entries across sessions and revisits the source turns of the linked facts to verify identities, resolve grouping errors, and recover details missing from the extracted facts. The resulting source-grounded entity representations or-ganize information across sessions while retaining links to the associated fact memories. At query time, the full configuration, ContextFact-E , retrieves both fact memories and entities and follows these links once: retrieved facts activate their entities, and the retrieved and activated entities surface related facts that retrieval alone would miss. ## 3 DIAGNOSING CONVERSATIONAL MEMORY 

End-to-end QA performance conflates several factors: what memory construction preserves, which evidence retrieval selects, and how effectively the reader uses the resulting context. To disentangle these effects, we design two controlled diagnostics. First, we eliminate retrieval selection by evalu-ating complete memory banks directly, comparing them against raw dialogue history and an oracle selection of supporting sessions. Second, we hold the source dialogue and unique-turn budget fixed while varying retrieval-unit boundaries, isolating how contextual grouping affects evidence access. Full diagnostic details and complete results are reported in Appx. E. 

Answerability after construction. We first remove retrieval selection by supplying each complete memory bank to the same reader. On LoCoMo with Qwen3.5-27B, all seven evaluated baseline banks fall below Full Context on single-hop questions (Fig. 2a), despite large differences in bank length. Thus, retrieval failure alone cannot account for the observed answerability gaps. Full-bank performance does not identify their cause—it reflects both what the constructed representation retains and how effectively the reader can use it. Fig. 2b illustrates two ways construction can make an answer harder or impossible to recover: omitting a queried detail, or preserving individual statements while losing the relation needed to interpret them. 

Selective access to retained evidence. Complete source dialogue avoids construction-time rewrit-ing, yet making the entire history available does not guarantee effective evidence use. As in Fig. 2c, Gold-Session—an oracle reference that retains only complete sessions containing supporting turns from the same history—improves over Full Context on every reported multi-hop/multi-session and temporal setting. The gains range from 4.61 to 17 .44 percentage points across the two benchmarks and readers. Since Gold-Session introduces no additional source information, this comparison shows that selectively exposing relevant evidence can be more effective than reading the complete history.

## 4 METHOD 

Guided by the design principle, we propose ContextFact , a two-view memory framework. The 

fact view preserves facts together with the context needed to interpret them, while the entity view 

reconstructs related evidence about recurring referents across sessions. As illustrated in Fig. 4, ContextFact first constructs these context-preserving fact memories and source-grounded entity rep-resentations (Sec. 4.1), then uses their links to support selective retrieval and readout (Sec. 4.2). 

Notation and memory objects. Let H = ( S1, . . . , S T ) be a conversation history of T sessions, where each St is an ordered sequence of conversational turns. ContextFact constructs a fact bank F,an entity bank E, and a provenance relation L linking records in the entity view to fact memories. Each fact memory also retains links to its source turns. Further notation is introduced as needed and summarized in Tab. 5. 4.1 CONTEXT -P RESERVING MEMORY CONSTRUCTION 

ContextFact constructs its memory in three main stages: (i) extracting context-preserving fact mem-ories and session-level entity entries, (ii) aligning recurring referents across sessions, and (iii) revis-iting source dialogue to reconstruct the entity view. The procedure is formalized in Algo. 1, with construction details in Appx. B (including the LLM instructions in Appx. B.1). 

Context-preserving fact extraction. ContextFact organizes each session around focal facts rather than fixed numbers of turns or pre-segmented topic blocks. Here, a focal fact serves as the organizing center that determines which supporting context is grouped with it. It concerns a specific occurrence, state, or intention described in the session, and it need not correspond to a single sentence or atomic proposition. For each focal fact, the extractor gathers the context needed to make it interpretable on its own, such as its circumstances, reasons, progress, or outcome, even when this context appears in nonadjacent turns. Consequently, each focal fact and its context forms a single fact memory, while distinct focal facts remain independently retrievable (Appx. G.1). 4Preprint Context-Preserving Fact Extraction

(a) ContextFact first extracts context-preserving fact memories and linked session-level entity entries from each dialogue session, followed by using provenace links to link the resulting fact and entities. At query time, both views are retrieved. A single entity-linked expansion allows retrieved facts to activate linked entities and expose additional related facts. Fact and entity candidates are then reranked separately, and those selected will be supplied to the reader. (b) Entity-view construction in detail. Session-level entity entries are incrementally aligned across sessions into provisional entities, organized into topic-based review groups, and reconstructed by revisiting their linked source dialogue. The resulting entities contain summaries and records linked to their supporting fact memories. See Appx. G for case studies. Before extraction, deterministic rules annotate supported relative-time expressions with absolute dates or intervals while preserving their original wording and granularity. A single LLM call then reads the complete annotated session eSt and jointly produces its fact memories and session-level entity entries . Each entry identifies a specific referent (such as a person, object, project, event, or place) with a name and a short description, linking it to the fact memories that substantively describe it. In addition, each fact memory retains its source-turn provenance. 

Cross-session entity binding. The extraction stage associates each session with entity entries de-scribing referents mentioned and linking them to the corresponding fact memories. Because the same underlying referent may be described differently across sessions, ContextFact incrementally aligns new entries with previously observed ones. Candidate matches are retrieved using dense sim-ilarity and BM25 (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009) over entry names and descriptions, after which a LLM determines whether a new entry refers to an existing entity or introduces a new one. En-tries judged to share the same referent are grouped into a provisional entity , providing a first-pass cross-session alignment while preserving the original entries and their fact links. 

Source-grounded entity reconstruction. Incremental binding can miss valid matches or incor-rectly combine distinct referents. After all sessions have been processed, ContextFact therefore performs a source-grounded reconstruction stage. Provisional entities are first grouped by topic to create local review groups; this grouping determines which candidates are reconsidered together but does not itself merge them. For each group, an LLM returns to the linked source dialogue (i.e., the linked conversational turns with deterministic temporal annotations) to verify referent identities and reconstruct the final entities. It may merge entries referring to the same entity, separate entries referring to distinct referents, and rebuild their summaries and records from source evidence. Each final entity is represented as e = ( ne, σ e, M(e)) , where ne is its name, σe is a concise sum-mary, and M(e) is a set of records. Each record m ∈ M (e) organizes a coherent aspect, event, state, or development of the entity, consolidating repeated or complementary evidence while keep-ing distinct occurrences and unresolved conflicts explicit. At query time, ContextFact proceeds in two stages: dual-view retrieval with a single entity-linked expansion, followed by independent reranking and readout. We describe the two stages below. The complete procedure is formalized in Algo. 2, with further details in Appx. C and illustrative cases in Appxs. G.2 and G.3. 

Dual-view retrieval and entity-linked expansion. Given a query q, hybrid dense and BM25 re-trieval with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF; Cormack et al., 2009) independently returns initial fact candidates from F and entity candidates from E. Fact memories are indexed using their memory text, while entity retrieval uses only their names ne and summaries σe. We then expand these can-didates through the existing provenance links in L. Retrieved facts can activate linked entities, and the resulting entity pool—including both directly retrieved and activated entities—can expose addi-tional facts through their records. This expansion is applied once, allowing direct matches to surface related cross-session evidence without recursive traversal. 

Independent reranking and readout. The expanded fact and entity candidates serve complemen-tary roles: fact memories provide focused evidence, whereas entity representations organize related evidence across sessions. We therefore rerank the two pools independently with a cross-encoder and retain the highest-ranked items from each view. Unlike seed retrieval, entity reranking uses the com-plete representation, including its name, summary, and all records. The selected complete entities are also supplied directly to the reader, following the selected fact memories and preserving rerank-ing order within each view. Thus, the entity view contributes in two ways: its links expose facts beyond direct query matches, while its complete representations provide organized cross-session evidence directly to the reader.

We next isolate three design choices in ContextFact: temporal normalization during memory con-struction, source-grounded entity reconstruction, and the two query-time roles of the entity view. The first two ablations vary memory construction while holding the downstream pipeline fixed; the third separates entity-linked expansion from direct entity readout. Full category-level results and controlled variants are reported in Appxs. F.3 to F.5. We then examine open-domain behavior and transfer across memory representations. 

Temporal normalization. ContextFact resolves supported relative-time expressions with deter-ministic rules before fact extraction, reducing reliance on joint LLM normalization. We compare rule-guided normalization with joint normalization and no temporal guidance. Rule-guided normal-ization gives the highest temporal J (Fig. 6a), improving over joint normalization by 3.74 points with both backbones. Inspection of the generated facts shows that joint normalization can introduce incorrect absolute dates, supporting rule-guided normalization before fact extraction.

Starting from the same provisional entity bank, we compare using the provisional entities directly, reconstructing from stored ContextFacts, and reconstructing from source dialogue. ## 6 CONCLUSION 

We study how to represent and organize history for selective long-term conversational memory. Our diagnostics show that answerability gaps can persist even when constructed memory banks are read in full, while retrieval-unit boundaries materially affect which supporting evidence reaches the reader. These findings motivate memory boundaries that follow the dependencies needed for interpretation, together with mechanisms for selectively reconnecting related evidence across ses-sions. ContextFact instantiates this principle through context-preserving fact memories and source-grounded entity representations, using the entity view for both entity-linked expansion and entity readout. Across LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S, ContextFact outperforms strong baselines while requiring substantially shorter evidence inputs, with controlled analyses confirming the individual contributions of fact construction, source reconstruction, and entity readout. Together, our results suggest that effective conversational memory requires not merely storing more information, but pre-serving the right context locally and reconnecting it selectively when needed. ## A RELATED WORK 

Conversational memory construction and granularity. Long-term memory systems vary in what they preserve and the units they expose for retrieval. MemoryBank combines dialogue his-tory with event summaries and user profiles (Zhong et al., 2024), MemoryOS organizes short-, mid-, and long-term stores (Kang et al., 2025), and Mem0 extracts and reconciles salient facts across interactions (Chhikara et al., 2025). LightMem combines compression with topic-aware consol-idation (Fang et al., 2026), while Nemori maintains episodic and semantic memories (Ma et al., 2026). Closer to our focus on memory boundaries, SeCom partitions dialogue into contiguous topi-cal segments (Pan et al., 2025), EMem constructs self-contained event-centric units with normalized entities, time, and source attribution (Zhou & Han, 2025), and SimpleMem constructs compact semantic memories and consolidates related information (Liu et al., 2026). ContextFact instead or-ganizes memory around focal facts: information that explains, qualifies, or develops the same fact may be gathered across nonadjacent turns, while distinct facts remain separately retrievable. Its boundaries are therefore determined by the context needed to interpret each fact rather than by fixed windows or contiguous topics. 

Linked memory and cross-session organization. Other work organizes memories beyond a flat collection. A-Mem links and updates contextualized notes (Xu et al., 2025), while Zep represents episodes, entities, relations, and temporal facts in a knowledge graph (Rasmussen et al., 2025). Memora connects concrete memories through primary abstractions and cue anchors (Xia et al., 2026); AtomMem organizes atomic facts into event structures and temporal profiles (Yao et al., 2026); TriMem combines raw dialogue, atomic facts, and synthesized profiles (Sun et al., 2026); and AnchorMem uses atomic facts as retrieval anchors while retaining associated source context (Shen et al., 2026). ContextFact likewise combines fine-grained and organized views, but constructs its entity view by aligning recurring referents across sessions and then revisiting source dialogue to verify identities and reconstruct entity-centered accounts. These accounts organize distinct aspects and developments, retain links to supporting fact memories, and are provided directly to the reader. 

Evidence retrieval and selection. Structured memories can also recover evidence beyond direct semantic matches. GraphRAG organizes entity and relation descriptions with community summaries (Edge et al., 2024); HippoRAG and HippoRAG 2 propagate relevance through graph associations (Gutiérrez et al., 2024; 2025); LinearRAG operates over an entity–sentence–passage graph (Zhuang et al., 2026); and EMem-G retrieves over its session–event–argument structure (Zhou & Han, 2025). Other systems use more adaptive query-time retrieval: Memora exploits memory connections with a policy (Xia et al., 2026), GAM performs query-time research over retained information (Yan et al., 2025), MemR 3 alternates retrieval and reflection (Du et al., 2025), and MRAgent iteratively tra-verses an associative memory graph (Ji et al., 2026). ContextFact instead uses a lightweight query-time procedure: independent fact and entity retrieval, a single entity-linked expansion, and separate reranking of the two candidate pools with MemReranker (Li et al., 2026). Selected facts provide focused evidence, while complete entity representations provide an organized cross-session view. 

Evaluating and diagnosing conversational memory. LoCoMo and LongMemEval evaluate memory over extended multi-session interactions (Maharana et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025), while long-context studies show that available evidence is not always used reliably (Liu et al., 2024). Recent work further separates sources of memory failure: Yuan et al. (2026) study retrieval and uti-lization bottlenecks across memory-writing and retrieval choices, while WhenLoss compares oracle evidence, complete stored memory, and retrieved memory to characterize write- and retrieval-side gaps (Yu et al., 2026). We complement these analyses with diagnostics focused on memory-unit design. Full-bank reading tests whether answerability gaps persist after removing retrieval selec-tion, while controlled retrieval-window interventions vary unit boundaries over unchanged dialogue at matched unique-turn budgets. ## D EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

This section provides the experimental details supporting Sec. 5. We describe the datasets and base-line systems, the implementation settings used for ContextFact, and the reader and judge protocols. Unless otherwise stated, the main QA comparisons use each method’s evaluated retrieval pipeline without a shared evidence-token cap. Evidence length counts only the memory text supplied to the reader. D.1 DATASETS 

LoCoMo (Maharana et al., 2024). LoCoMo contains extended conversations between two speak-ers, generated from personas and temporal event graphs and subsequently verified by human annota-tors. Its question-answering task requires recalling details and connecting evidence across sessions. We evaluate the ten-conversation subset, excluding adversarial questions, for a total of 1,540 ques-tions: 841 single-hop, 321 temporal, 282 multi-hop, and 96 open-domain. Single-hop questions target individual facts, multi-hop questions combine multiple pieces of evidence, temporal ques-tions concern dates and event order, and open-domain questions connect conversational information with background knowledge. 

LongMemEval-S (Wu et al., 2025). LongMemEval evaluates memory over timestamped user– assistant histories, covering information extraction, cross-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention when the history does not support an answer. We evaluate all 500 questions in LongMemEval-S: 70 single-user, 56 single-assistant, 133 multi-session, 133 tem-poral, 78 knowledge-update, and 30 single-preference. Unlike LoCoMo’s recurring speaker pairs, these histories contain accumulated user interactions, including assistant responses and revisions to previously stated facts. D.2 BASELINES 

We compare ContextFact with a full-history reference (Full Context), a retrieval baseline (RAG), and representative conversational-memory systems spanning segmented, fact-based, episodic, and structured memory representations. The descriptions below emphasize the memory representation and retrieval mechanism relevant to our comparison: • SeCom (Pan et al., 2025) . SeCom partitions conversations into topically coherent segments and applies compression-based denoising to construct memory units. It retrieves relevant segments using their compressed representations to support response generation. • Mem0 (Chhikara et al., 2025) . Mem0 extracts salient facts from incoming dialogue using recent exchanges and historical summaries. It reconciles these facts with stored memories through addition, update, deletion, or no-change operations, and retrieves relevant facts for answering. 24 Preprint • A-Mem (Xu et al., 2025). A-Mem represents interactions as structured notes enriched with contextual descriptions, keywords, and tags. It links related notes and updates existing memory as new information arrives, supporting retrieval over contextualized memory units. • MemoryOS (Kang et al., 2025). MemoryOS organizes conversations into short-term, mid-term, and long-term memory, with consolidation and updating across levels. Its reader input combines retrieved dialogue memories with longer-term user knowledge and profile information. • LightMem (Fang et al., 2026). LightMem compresses incoming dialogue and groups re-lated content through topic segmentation before extracting memories. It separates online memory accumulation from offline consolidation and retrieves stored memories by seman-tic similarity. • AtomMem (Yao et al., 2026). AtomMem introduces a high-quality atomic fact extraction dataset produced through model-based extraction and human refinement. Its memory sys-tem organizes atomic facts into hierarchical event structures and temporal profiles, with an associative memory graph connecting related information. • SimpleMem (Liu et al., 2026). SimpleMem compresses dialogue into self-contained mem-ory units by resolving references, anchoring temporal expressions, and synthesizing related information. These units are indexed for query-dependent retrieval to construct the answer-ing context. • EMem (Zhou & Han, 2025). EMem decomposes conversations into enriched elementary discourse units: self-contained event statements with normalized entities, temporal cues, and source-turn attributions. It combines dense retrieval with recall-oriented LLM filtering to select relevant units for answering. • Nemori (Ma et al., 2026). Nemori segments conversations into coherent episodes and main-tains complementary episodic and semantic memories. Its construction framework uses prediction and calibration to derive knowledge from episodes, while retrieval draws on both memory types. • Memora (Xia et al., 2026). Memora organizes factual and episodic memories under pri-mary abstractions and connects them through cue anchors. Its semantic retriever (S) selects memories by similarity, while its policy retriever (P) uses an LLM to navigate the memory structure and gather related evidence. D.3 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS

F.8 GENERALIZABILITY OF THE ENTITY VIEW ACROSS MEMORY SYSTEMS 

To assess whether the entity view transfers across memory representations, we augment EMem’s event units (Zhou & Han, 2025), SeCom’s contextual segments (Pan et al., 2025), and SimpleMem’s self-contained facts (Liu et al., 2026). For each system, we retain its existing memory bank, construct an entity view over its units, and apply our retrieval pipeline to select original units and entity evi-dence ( +Entity ). ## H FAILURE ANALYSIS OF THE ENTITY VIEW 

Adding the entity view lowers open-domain judge accuracy from 64 .58% to 59 .38% with GPT-4.1-mini and from 54 .17% to 51 .04% with Qwen3.5-27B. Among the 96 open-domain questions, nine Qwen3.5-27B answers change from accepted under ContextFact-F to rejected under ContextFact-E, while six change in the opposite direction. The corresponding counts for GPT-4.1-mini are ten and five. We refer to these judge-defined changes as regressions and gains, respectively. We examine whether fact expansion accounts for the aggregate decline (Appx. H.1), audit the nine Qwen3.5-27B regressions against the source dialogue and reader inputs (Appx. H.2), and present all nine cases (Appx. H.3). The audit distinguishes observed answer patterns from causal explanations and examines whether another reader can recover the benchmark answer from the same evidence. H.1 DOES FACT EXPANSION EXPLAIN THE OPEN -D OMAIN DROP ?ContextFact-E changes both the selected fact evidence and the reader input. Entity-linked expansion enlarges the candidate pool before reranking, so the selected ContextFacts can differ from those in ContextFact-F. The reader also receives ten selected entities. We thus examine evidence-turn recall and controlled ablations to distinguish the effects of changing the fact list and adding entity readout. 

Evidence-turn recall. We measure the fraction of annotated supporting turns cited by at least one selected ContextFact, averaged over questions with evidence annotations. Entity-linked expansion retains the original candidates and adds linked facts, although reranking can still replace individual selected facts. In Tab. 20, recall increases in every question category. These aggregate results indicate broader coverage of annotated supporting turns.
